**Question:** How closely do two related operational definitions agree on identical beats?

**Dataset/sample:** 18 participants and 1724 paired valid beats, Post-exercise; selected pair X5/X6 shares the same waveform-threshold family.

**Source data:** [F09_IITB_X_X5_X6_bland_altman_post_exercise.csv](fig09_iitb_x_x5_x6_bland_altman_post_exercise.csv) and the canonical tables identified in the calculation.

**Calculation:** Difference = algorithm A minus B; bias and empirical limits use equal participant weights, with clustered participant bootstrap uncertainty in RQ1_bland_altman_summary.csv. The left panel preserves all observations; the right panel repeats the same data over pooled 1st–99th-percentile x/y limits.

**Axes:** Both panels — X: mean X5/X6 timing relative to ECG R (ms). Y: signed X5 − X6 difference (ms).

**Result:** Participant-balanced bias was 23.61 ms (95% CI 17.37 to 30.49); limits of agreement were -77.88 to 125.11 ms.

**Interpretation:** The selected threshold definitions can be systematically offset even when both return valid events.

**Limitation:** Bland–Altman agreement is not independent-reference accuracy; repeated-beat limits depend on the participant-balanced empirical model.

**RQ1 contribution:** Quantifies method interchangeability for a justified pair without exhaustively screening arbitrary pairs.
