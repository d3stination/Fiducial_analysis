# Scientific findings

HeartCycle and ReBeat support reference comparisons, but they answer different questions. HeartCycle compares B and X timing with ECHO AVO and AVC. ReBeat compares methods with expert ICG annotations. IITB provides condition and reliability evidence without an independent B/X timing reference.

Reference rank did not identify a universal best method. HeartCycle B8 had the lowest observed participant-median B absolute error against ECHO AVO at 12.59 ms. This does not generalise to X, to ReBeat expert annotations, or to recordings with different acquisition and preprocessing. Coverage and error tails remain part of the comparison.

Detection failures changed the interpretation of retained-beat disagreement. X common-valid samples were smaller because several X definitions failed or became invalid on difficult beats. A lower X disagreement estimate among retained beats is therefore not evidence of higher reliability by itself.

Paired condition results are participant-specific. IITB B1 and B7 failure increased after exercise even where complete-case timing disagreement decreased. ReBeat baseline-to-cognitive changes differed by method and outcome. These results do not isolate physiology from motion, acquisition context, RR change or waveform quality.

The full six-feature morphology screen contains 1,794 tests. The paired condition-morphology family contains 672 tests and 14 associations that met its exploratory bootstrap-direction and BH criteria. Unsupported and unavailable results remain in the canonical tables.

The IITB X3 beat-to-beat variability versus failure-change result is unsupported. The corrected values are rho 0.39736, exact two-sided permutation p 0.11111, q 0.96044 and n=18. Seventeen participants had no failure-rate change. The positive bootstrap interval is conditional on the resamples that retained outcome variation and does not override the permutation result.

Verified failure reasons describe algorithm execution, not physiological abnormality. No qualifying feature, unusable window and boundary rejection can explain why a frozen definition returned no accepted point. They do not prove that morphology caused the failure.
